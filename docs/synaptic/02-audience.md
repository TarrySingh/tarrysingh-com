# 02 · Audience

Three concrete personas. Build for these — not for everyone.

---

## Persona A — Dr. Marta Brunetti, EIC Pathfinder panellist

- 58, Italian, neuroscience-and-engineering background, two ERC Advanced Grants.
- Reads 40 proposals a cycle, on trains and in airport lounges.
- Decides on the first page whether to advocate, abstain, or attack.
- Will Google a PI's name and look at the top three results before reading their submitted Part B.
- **What makes her advocate:** evidence of a *consortium that has thought hard*. Concrete decision milestones. Names she trusts on the team. No marketing veneer.
- **What makes her abstain:** marketing veneer, unclear failure modes, vague success thresholds.

**Implications for the site:**
- Every proposal page must show the five-objective grid (O1–O5) with success thresholds and decision milestones **above the fold on a 13-inch MacBook**.
- Partner names and one-line credentials must be visible without scrolling on the cover.
- The site must look authored, not commissioned. Marta should never feel like she is on a marketing site.

---

## Persona B — Tom Reeves, Deep-Tech Partner at a London / Berlin VC

- 41, ex-DeepMind product, now writes $5M–$15M cheques at seed/Series A.
- Glances at every founder once via a friend's link. Decides in <3 minutes whether to ask for a meeting.
- Knows the SWE-bench reproducibility story; knows neuromorphic is hard; is suspicious of both spaces.
- **What makes him email:** a thesis statement he hadn't formed himself, supported by names he respects, with a concrete decision tree.
- **What makes him close the tab:** generic "AI for X" framing, missing physics/biology mechanism, no skin-in-the-game commitments.

**Implications for the site:**
- The SYMPHONY page must clearly articulate **why current LLM coding agents are structurally capped**, with the SWE-bench re-evaluation numbers visible.
- The MEMPHIS page must clearly articulate **why memristive crossbars + replay** is a non-obvious combination — not a generic "brain-inspired chip".
- There must be a one-line invitation to a 30-minute call **at the bottom of each proposal page**, not in a sticky CTA bar.

---

## Persona C — Dr. Anjali Rao, peer scientist (neuro / systems / AI4code)

- 35, postdoc transitioning to faculty, well-followed on Bluesky/X.
- Reads everything in her field, will share what she respects.
- Wants to see the science, not the press release. Will dismiss anything she can't trace to a primary source.
- **What makes her share:** a phrase she wishes she'd written, or a diagram she wishes she'd drawn.
- **What makes her unfollow:** AI-generated illustrations, citation theatre, overclaiming.

**Implications for the site:**
- The plates must be original. Every figure must be attributable to a human design hand. No DALL-E / Midjourney visuals.
- Citations must be inline, hover-revealable, with DOI links where possible.
- Each proposal must include a **"Critical uncertainty"** card that names what could still fail. This is the single most important credibility-builder for Anjali.

---

## Anti-personas (do not optimise for these)

- **Sneha, the curious LinkedIn scroller.** Will share the page on LinkedIn with three fire emojis. Not our audience. Do not lower the floor of the language for her.
- **Greg, the engineering recruiter.** Will skim for "currently hiring". Don't make him stay. There is no jobs page.
- **The crypto-VC summer intern.** Will not understand the page. That is fine.

---

## Reading paths to optimise

1. **Direct link to `/memphis` from an EIC reviewer's email.** Must be self-orienting in 5 seconds.
2. **Tweet / Bluesky post linking to `/symphony` with the SWE-bench framing.** Must reward Anjali with a citation block and a falsifiability box.
3. **Cover URL pasted into a VC partner Slack channel.** Must show two plate-card hero images that look unmistakably like the same studio's work, then a single italic line.
