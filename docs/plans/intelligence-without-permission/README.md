## The argument

PhD-level intellectual work is becoming available outside the institutions that historically concentrated it. As capabilities spread, a small organisation can potentially command research capacity far beyond what its headcount suggests. Universities and enterprises must then defend the value they create through experiments, judgement, facilities, reliable products and new discoveries. Their ability to concentrate exceptional people becomes a less exclusive advantage.

The central challenge is to both academia and corporate R&D. What happens when smaller labs can coordinate very large populations of capable research agents against work associated with ASML, IBM, Google or Dyson? A challenger need not reproduce an entire incumbent. Winning a valuable research problem, a component, a design tool or a recurring service can change who captures the return.

The article should make this prospect vivid while establishing what has already happened, what is becoming accessible, and what remains a conditional future. “Millions of PhD-level agents” is the scenario to investigate. It is not a claim that a million autonomous human-equivalent researchers can currently be bought with an ordinary subscription.

The target of criticism is a demonstrable institutional assumption: that unusually capable intellectual labour will remain difficult for outsiders to assemble. Criticise decisions, incentives and claims of exclusivity. Do not invent motives or describe named organisations as complacent without evidence.

Three propositions carry the whole essay:

1. **Access changes participation.** People previously excluded by geography, credentials, hiring budgets or institutional affiliation can attempt more ambitious intellectual work.
2. **Coordination changes organisational scale.** A small human team may organise many research attempts, but useful output depends on verification, diversity, cost and the structure of the problem.
3. **Competition changes where value sits.** As some intellectual tasks become less scarce, experimental capacity, unique observations, manufacturing reliability, distribution and control of compute can become more valuable.

The conclusion must remain forceful even if a particular announcement is revised. Its foundation is the accumulated change in who can do useful research, not the fate of a single famous theorem.

## What counts as PhD-level capability

Use the user's phrase prominently, then define it in terms of work: reading technical literature, constructing and testing hypotheses, writing scientific software, analysing measurements, building mathematical arguments and revising them after criticism. A doctorate also develops judgement, field knowledge and practical research skills that cannot be inferred from an examination score.

Evaluate each case across five separate questions:

- What task did the system complete, and how demanding was it?
- What did humans, specialist software, existing papers and experimental teams contribute?
- Who checked the result, with what method?
- Can an outsider access the relevant model and tools, in their country and field?
- What is the complete cost of obtaining a useful, checked result?

Keep capability, access, affordability and reliability distinct throughout. An internal frontier result can demonstrate direction without proving that the same result is available to the public today. Public access and a company's stock-market listing are also different events; the argument does not require an IPO.

## Four enterprise case studies

Apply the same questions to each company so the comparison has substance. These are research hypotheses, not conclusions that the companies have already lost their advantages.

| Company | Work a smaller lab could challenge | Advantages requiring separate evidence | Incumbent response to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|
| ASML | Computational lithography, calibration, metrology software, component research and selected optimisation problems | Optics, fabrication tolerances, suppliers, installed-base observations, reliability, qualification and service | AI in R&D, external partnerships, software integration and the Mistral relationship |
| IBM | Materials screening, scientific software, algorithm development and particular research services | Experimental equipment, customer relationships, proprietary observations, engineering validation and deployment | Its own accelerated-discovery work and released materials models |
| Google | Individual algorithmic discoveries and specialised research products built using available models | Compute, data, distribution, infrastructure and the ability to internalise improvements | Google is simultaneously an incumbent, an enabler of challengers and a beneficiary of automated research |
| Dyson | Motor optimisation, airflow, acoustics, sensing, control software and design-space exploration | Prototyping, materials behaviour, safety, manufacturability, reliability, tooling and distribution | Existing AI/ML research, external partnerships and integration into physical product development |

For every case, identify a concrete unit of output, a realistic customer, an incumbent process, a challenger route, a validation requirement and a route to revenue. Avoid treating revenue, patent counts, R&D spend or employee numbers as interchangeable measures of scientific capacity.

## The million-agent thought experiment

Build an explicit model readers can inspect. Let them vary the number of concurrent workers, work duration, task cost, independent useful contribution, coordination overhead, checker capacity and experimental capacity. Display the assumptions beside the result. Never multiply a human salary by an agent count and label the result “research value”.

A deliberately simple explanatory model can express accepted output per interval as the smallest of candidate production, verification capacity and experimental capacity. Candidate production itself depends on the number of attempts and a stated rate of useful, non-duplicate results. Every input must carry units. Correlation and serial dependencies require sensitivity analysis; a linear scaling assumption cannot silently fill the gap.

The important comparison is **cost and time per independently checked result**. Headcount and token volume provide context. They do not measure discovery. Compare small human teams using AI with incumbents also using AI, at both equal compute budgets and plausible unequal budgets.

Include three outcomes: the smaller lab wins a narrow task; the incumbent integrates the technology and widens its lead; neither gains because experiments, coordination or reliable measurement dominate. These are testable alternatives. Their relative likelihood must come from evidence rather than the desired ending.

## What “10× better” means in production

Treat the phrase as the quality ambition, not a measurable claim about the finished work. The improvement should be visible in the following properties:

| Dimension | Required improvement and acceptance condition |
|---|---|
| Original argument | A clear explanation of how widely available research capability changes institutional authority and corporate R&D, including mechanisms that could preserve incumbent power. |
| Prose | Each chapter adds evidence or reasoning; no chapter survives merely by repeating the central warning. Explain necessary scientific terms on first use. Remove slogans, filler and formulaic transitions. |
| Accuracy | Every consequential factual claim maps to a source and a dated evidence status. Company reports are identified as company reports. Predictions are labelled as predictions. |
| Attribution | Credit prior mathematics, public datasets, open software, human direction and experimental work. The essay must not erase the institutions on whose work AI builds. |
| Scientific visuals | Coordinates and units are traceable. Animations explain a specific relationship. A picture of a vortex is never described as proof of a singularity. |
| Economic visuals | Measured data and scenarios are visibly separated. Readers can inspect definitions, assumptions, dates and uncertainty. |
| Interaction | Every control changes a meaningful parameter or reveals a specific piece of evidence. Each figure has reset, keyboard access and a static explanation. |
| Reading experience | Full narrative remains readable without interaction. Chapters have stable links, a usable mobile contents list, accurate progress and a return-to-reading position. |
| Performance | Progressive activation; inactive 3D views stop rendering and release resources. Essential text and static figures load before optional 3D. |
| Verification | Report the actual word count, distinct figure count, data checks, build results and accessibility/performance findings. No “ready” claim before these checks finish. |

Use prose that is direct enough to sting. Prefer a documented decision and its cost to a paragraph calling an institution slow. Avoid unsupported extinction predictions, manufactured anecdotes, invented quotations and assumptions that all people or industries benefit equally.

## Research and editorial method

For every case, create a record containing the problem; baseline; result; scope; date; source; author affiliations; model and tool contribution; human contribution; cost where known; access conditions; verification status; limitation; and institutional implication. Preserve a field for the strongest contrary evidence.

Evidence states should distinguish an announcement, a paper or preprint, a reported formal artefact, independently reproduced computation, an experimental result, deployment and measured economic outcomes. These are not a universal linear ladder: formal mathematics and clinical evidence require different checks.

Build a broad source base from original papers, formal repositories, independent scientific assessment, public datasets, company filings and official statistics. Coverage and relevance determine the final bibliography; a large citation count is not a substitute for substantiation. The initial reviewed sources and unresolved work are in [research-ledger.md](research-ledger.md).

Include a deliberate challenge to the thesis in every institutional section. Universities produce the datasets and methods AI uses. Incumbents can access the same tools. Some research is limited by expensive experiments rather than ideas. Many agents can reproduce the same error. Widely distributed access can still depend on a handful of providers. Address these directly rather than burying them in a caveat section.

The repository contains an earlier essay, “Your AI Doesn't Discover Anything. Here's the Math That Proves It.” Revisit its distinction between recombination, conventional scientific novelty and changes in representation. Do not quietly retain incompatible claims, or pretend that a particular theoretical definition settles every empirical question about AI discovery. The new essay should show the reader exactly what evidence changed and which distinctions still matter.

## Production sequence

1. **Research dossiers and claim map.** Establish the factual basis for all chapters, current availability and contrary evidence. Resolve the central mathematical distinctions before prose depends on them.
2. **One finished representative section.** Write a complete 2,400–3,000-word chapter and implement three representative figures: one mathematical 3D view, one measured 2D comparison and the agent-lab scenario. Use this to establish the prose, scientific explanation and visual standard.
3. **Complete manuscript.** Write all chapters against the argument map; reconcile terminology and chronology; run a dedicated cut for repetition; reach at least 40,000 qualifying words after editing.
4. **Complete figure production.** Build all 38 distinct figures from the storyboard. Every figure receives a data/method note, accessible description and verified fallback. Reuse rendering infrastructure without repeating the same visual argument.
5. **Integrate the reading experience.** Assemble the canonical article, contents, sources, progress, deep links and print behaviour. Confirm rendering and navigation on long chapters.
6. **Adversarial review and verification.** Check scientific attribution, availability, proof status, quantitative comparisons, units, scenario labels and the strongest counterarguments. Validate word count, figures, links, type checking, build, accessibility and performance.
7. **Prepare publication.** Recheck fast-moving claims against the publication date, resolve local/live divergence, and produce the final reviewable article through the site's existing release workflow. Publishing and any distribution are separate actions from this planning deliverable.

No shorter article, placeholder figure, inaccessible 3D canvas, repeated content or inflated word-count convention may stand in for completing the commission.

## Opening voice study

The following is an editorial specimen, not a reported incident or a passage counted towards the manuscript.

> A research department is partly a way of gathering people who are difficult to find. It puts their desks near one another, gives them equipment, pays them long enough to fail, and hopes that something valuable emerges from the conversation. The building matters. The instruments matter. For many institutions, so does the belief that this concentration of intelligence is difficult for anyone else to reproduce.
>
> That belief is becoming a question.
>
> Consider a small laboratory competing with a much larger one. Its advantage would once have needed to come from an exceptional founder, a neglected problem or a method the incumbent had overlooked. Now imagine that the small lab can call on thousands of capable research agents, direct them towards different approaches, and check the promising results. Increase the number again. The payroll tells you very little about how much intellectual work the organisation can attempt.
>
> Whether those attempts produce good science is the central test. A thousand copies of an error are still an error. An elegant design can fail on the workbench. A proof can establish something narrower than its announcement suggests. But if the results survive those tests, the incumbent faces a competitor its organisational chart has taught it to underestimate.
>
> This is the question behind the recent scientific announcements. Who can now attempt work that once required an invitation into a particular room? And what must the people who control that room offer when exceptional intellectual assistance becomes available outside it?

*The right to attempt serious work is becoming harder to reserve for the people already inside.*
