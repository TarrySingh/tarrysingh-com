# Five industries after the research bottleneck

Chapter 12 brief, updated 9 September 2026. Planned length: 3,200 narrative words within the 40,000-word total. These are conditional future scenarios to develop from evidence, not predictions presented as established outcomes.

The chapter should answer what better industry looks like when advanced intellectual assistance becomes widely available. The consequences include stronger incumbents, new specialist competitors, cheaper services and more ambitious work. Displacement is one possible outcome; improved products and wider participation are equally central.

## 1. Oil and gas — 700 words

**Present constraint to investigate:** specialised interpretations, incompatible observations, model uncertainty and the time needed to turn operating data into a useful decision. Acknowledge existing AI and computational practice. The IEA describes oil and gas as an early adopter; calling the sector untouched would weaken the essay. See [IEA's energy analysis](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/ai-for-energy-optimisation-and-innovation).

**Future scene:** a smaller engineering lab brings reservoir modelling, equipment analysis and emissions measurement into one reviewable work programme. Agents compare interpretations and test assumptions; qualified engineers inspect the evidence before changing operations.

**How a company benefits:** more alternatives evaluated before an expensive decision, better maintenance planning, clearer uncertainty and faster investigation of possible leaks. A new entrant may sell a narrow analytical service while an incumbent integrates the capability across its asset base.

**What remains difficult:** reliable field measurements, geological uncertainty, equipment access, operating responsibility, safety and the cost of physical interventions. Cheaper analysis alone does not establish lower total emissions or better investment outcomes.

**Evidence of improvement:** decision time, predictive calibration, false alarms, unplanned downtime and independently measured emissions outcomes. Avoid invented percentage gains.

## 2. Pharmaceuticals and biotech — 500 words

**Present constraint to investigate:** expensive coordination between literature, structure prediction, design, experimental results and candidate selection.

**Future scene:** a small research company uses available models and specialist tools to compare a larger set of candidates, then contracts independent laboratory testing. The experimental results inform its next research choices. Keep all biological discussion at the level needed to explain the economics and validation, without procedural laboratory instructions.

**How companies and patients could benefit:** more useful hypotheses tested, less scientist time spent on repetitive analysis, better documented negative results and a broader population able to originate research. Existing pharmaceutical companies can use the same tools to improve internal decisions and assess outside discoveries.

**What remains difficult:** reproducibility, toxicity, efficacy, clinical trials, manufacturing, patient diversity and access to treatment. A binding result is not a medicine.

**Evidence of improvement:** independently reproduced experimental findings, cost per validated candidate, trial outcomes and ultimately patient benefit. The [AlphaFold 3 paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07487-w) and the original protein-design sources establish upstream capabilities, not those downstream outcomes.

## 3. Advanced manufacturing and industrial design — 500 words

**Present constraint to investigate:** design exploration spread across mechanical, thermal, electrical, acoustic and manufacturing teams, with slow hand-offs and costly prototypes.

**Future scene:** a small engineering lab explores competing motor or component designs with explicit performance and manufacturing constraints. It supplies checked designs or analysis tools to manufacturers, or builds a narrowly focused product of its own.

**How a company benefits:** more design options examined, earlier identification of trade-offs, reusable test evidence and faster engineering decisions. Incumbents can combine this intellectual capacity with existing factories and real operating data.

**What remains difficult:** material variability, tolerances, tooling, durability, qualification, suppliers and high-volume yield. A simulated optimum can still be a poor product.

**Evidence of improvement:** independently tested efficiency, noise, reliability, yield, prototype count and total development cost under matched requirements. The ASML and Dyson cases in Chapter 11 supply concrete context; this section advances from their advantages to possible operating models.

## 4. Energy and utilities — 500 words

**Present constraint to investigate:** complex planning across generation, grids, storage, maintenance and demand, with sparse measurements in some areas and constraints that change over time.

**Future scene:** a municipal utility or regional engineering team can commission a level of planning analysis once associated with a much larger organisation. Agents develop and challenge possible schedules and investments using the same documented network constraints.

**How a company and community could benefit:** clearer trade-offs, better use of existing assets, more effective maintenance and the ability to evaluate additional storage or demand-management options. Smaller utilities gain analytical capacity; larger utilities can expand the number of scenarios they scrutinise.

**What remains difficult:** network physics, reliability, actual asset condition, permissions, interconnection queues, equipment delivery and construction. An improved plan does not install a transformer.

**Evidence of improvement:** forecast calibration, constraint violations, curtailment, reliability and delivered project cost. Potential efficiency gains must be distinguished from the electricity consumed by the AI systems themselves.

## 5. Enterprise software and technical services — 500 words

**Present constraint to investigate:** scarce specialist time for bespoke analysis, software changes, integration, testing and ongoing support.

**Future scene:** a small technical firm combines research agents with executable tests and domain experts to deliver a narrow service previously requiring a larger consultancy or vendor team. The customer buys a validated result with clear responsibility for maintaining it.

**How companies and customers could benefit:** more affordable custom tools, shorter queues for changes, greater competition and more work that becomes economical to undertake. Incumbents can use the same capability to improve their service and reconsider how they charge for it.

**What remains difficult:** understanding messy systems, integration, security, data access, contractual responsibility, uptime and the quality of acceptance tests.

**Evidence of improvement:** accepted tasks per unit of total cost, defect and rollback rates, customer retention, integration time and actual pricing. A better coding benchmark is upstream evidence, not proof that a firm's business model has already collapsed.

## Comparison and bridge — 500 words

The five futures differ because the expensive step differs. Digital tests can sometimes be repeated quickly. Biological experiments, qualification and industrial work often cannot. Trace both the falling cost of research assistance and the capacity of the system that must accept, test or deploy its output.

Give each sector two plausible branches: an incumbent adopts successfully; a smaller challenger wins a specific part of the value chain. Show a third outcome where increased analytical activity produces little benefit because the remaining constraint is not relieved. Do not assign dates or probabilities without supporting evidence.

End with what these industries share: organisations may be able to attempt more useful work with fewer limits imposed by access to expertise. Whether that becomes cheaper products, better science or additional profit depends on competition and on the ability to turn analysis into results.
