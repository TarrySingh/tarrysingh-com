# Security self-assessment  (≤ 5,000 chars)  ·  3,646

SYMPHONY has been reviewed with regard to security, export control, and dual-use considerations in accordance with Horizon Europe and EIC Pathfinder requirements.

The project does not involve EU Classified Information (EUCI), classified background information, or activities requiring national or EU-level security clearance. No sensitive information subject to security restrictions is generated, accessed, or processed during the project.

The research focuses on fundamental scientific investigation and early-stage technology development (TRL 1–4) in the area of neuromimetic knowledge representation for software systems. The models, algorithms, and software prototypes developed are intended exclusively for civilian research purposes, including code comprehension, software maintenance, engineer onboarding, and equitable access to software-engineering tools.

The demonstrator codebases used to validate the substrate are openly licensed open-source systems (selected at M3 from a shortlist of Linux-kernel subsystems, PostgreSQL, and Kubernetes) and a non-sensitive industrial-automation codebase contributed by UP Robotics under controlled disclosure. None of these codebases contains classified material, cryptographic primitives, weapons-related logic, or information subject to national security restrictions.

No dual-use items, as defined under Regulation (EU) 2021/821 on export controls, are involved. The project does not develop cryptographic systems, surveillance infrastructure, weapons-related software, or technologies with inherent military or intelligence applications. The neuromimetic substrate is a general-purpose code-comprehension representation; it does not target, decode, or analyse sensitive systems. The user-study component (≥ 60 engineers, run under Newcastle ethics approval) involves only voluntary participation on non-sensitive demonstrator code, with informed consent and GDPR-compliant data handling.

The consortium includes no military, defence, intelligence, or security-sector partners and receives no defence-related funding. There is no credible pathway for the project results to be applied to military use, mass surveillance, internal repression, or other security-sensitive contexts. SYMPHONY's narrow, auditable low-bandwidth task-control interface is specifically engineered to bound the substrate's behaviour under operational deployment, in contrast to the unbounded prompt-response surface of current large-language-model agents, and is therefore inherently amenable to civilian governance.

All scientific outputs are intended for open dissemination through peer-reviewed open-access publications, FAIR research-data repositories, and open-source software releases, subject to standard intellectual property management. This open-science approach is incompatible with classified or restricted applications and reinforces the civilian and non-sensitive nature of the research.

The project's relationship to the EU Artificial Intelligence Act has been considered. SYMPHONY's outputs constitute general-purpose AI research at TRL 1–4 and do not develop, deploy, or supply an AI system within the scope of the Act's high-risk classifications. Any future deployment beyond the project's research scope would be subject to a separate compliance assessment under the applicable provisions of the Act.

No additional security risks have been identified. This self-assessment has been reviewed and confirmed by the Project Coordinator and all consortium partners, who will continue to monitor compliance with EU security, ethics, and export-control regulations throughout the project lifecycle.
