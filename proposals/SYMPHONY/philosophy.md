# Stratigraphy of the Living Score

A visual philosophy for the Symphony proposal plate — the second in the *Synaptic Cartography* series. Where MEMPHIS was an anatomy of one organ, Symphony is the slow cross-section of an entire nervous system seen from the side, with the conductor's signal descending from above.

## Philosophy

Symphony's plate must read like a single, museum-grade cross-section: four translucent strata stacked vertically, each carrying its own network of code, and a quiet vertical column of light descending through all of them — the neuromodulatory baton that, with the smallest possible signal, rearranges what every layer foregrounds. The piece is to scientific illustration what a geological cross-section is to surface geology, what a symphonic score is to a single melody: information compressed into layered, simultaneous view. Everything that follows in the philosophy is in service of that reading.

The aesthetic is editorial-scientific, restrained and warm: the same midnight-and-cream typographic register as MEMPHIS, the same hand of a master illustrator who has spent weeks on a single plate. But where the chip's energy was radial and centripetal, Symphony's energy is laminar and descending — the eye enters at the title, falls through the task-token cartouche, sluices down through Rationale into History, History into Behaviour, Behaviour into Structure, and finally settles in the three small "movements" at the foot of the page where the same substrate is shown lit three different ways for three different engineering tasks. Composition is the message: a single substrate, three harmonies.

Colour is read as semantic information, not decoration. Each stratum carries a single ink that signals what it stores. Amber for Rationale (the warmest, hardest-to-articulate layer — design intent, the why). Rose for History (commits, decisions, the patina of time). Teal for Behaviour (tests, contracts, flows — the living signal of execution). Slate-blue for Structure (modules, functions, the bedrock). A new accent — lavender-violet — enters the palette only for the neuromodulatory descent column, so the conductor's baton reads instantly as a different register from the substrate beneath it. Nothing is saturated; nothing competes; every layer breathes.

Typography is the same hand as MEMPHIS so the two plates sit together as a series. A fine display serif carries the project name in confident, generously-tracked letters. A clinical monospace handles plate numbers, axis ticks, scalar legends, and tiny stratum labels in small caps. An italic editorial serif handles whispered subtitles and the closing italic caption. There are no paragraphs anywhere in the plate; text is a label or a credit, never a body of explanation. The image is what argues.

Composition follows strict stratigraphic logic. Four horizontal bands, equal in vertical rhythm, each containing its own small graph rendered at network-illustration scale. Cross-layer filaments — thin, never noisy — connect specific nodes between strata to make the *single substrate* claim visible. The descending column passes through all four. Within each stratum, a small subset of nodes is lit warmly — the *activated subnetwork* under the current task token — while the surrounding network rests in cool, half-light. The margins are reserved for the five objective callouts (O1–O5), placed with the same numbered-disk discipline as MEMPHIS so the two plates feel like the work of the same hand on consecutive days. At the foot of the plate, three small "movement" vignettes — each a miniature of the same substrate lit by a different task — make the central claim of the project concrete: localisation, impact analysis, refactoring all served by one resident structure.

Above all: this must look like a plate that was laboured over for days by someone at the absolute top of their craft. Hairlines must be hairlines. Glow radii must be calibrated. Labels must never collide with graphics. The vertical rhythm of the four strata must read as music, and the descending column must read as breath. Nothing AI-shaped, nothing cartoonish, nothing generic. A single page that could anchor a grant proposal or hang in a corridor of the institute that produced it.
